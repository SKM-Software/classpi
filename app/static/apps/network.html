Teaching notes

Plaintext: anyone in the middle reads it. Classical ciphers (Caesar, Vigenere, Pigpen) scramble the text, but the middle can still crack them - the "Crack it" button on the middle screen shows how (brute-forcing 25 Caesar shifts, or reading pigpen off the key card). This is why they are not used for real security. Modern encryption (AES) needs a secret key that never travels, so the middle is stuck with gibberish.

A nice point for pupils (Kerckhoffs's principle): the algorithm can be public knowledge - what keeps a message safe is the secrecy of the key, not the method.

The middle Pi here is an honest relay you route through on purpose, so the whole demo runs safely on your own kit. Real attacks (e.g. ARP spoofing) force traffic through an attacker without consent - worth naming for pupils, but not something to run on a live network.
